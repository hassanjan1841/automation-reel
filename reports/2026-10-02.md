What the strong openings share: Across both groups the visual style is nearly identical (bold sans-serif headline, one or two words underlined in orange, small red/orange eyebrow label, @hassanjan.k signature), so layout and typography aren't what separates them. The difference is in what the headline text promises: the strong covers ("Why is your Supabase query so slow?", "Over 16,000 Supabase databases have publicly readable tables") name a concrete tool/number and pose a direct, personal problem ("your query") that implies a specific fix or exposed risk is coming. The weak covers either ask a vaguer, more reflective question with no named tool or artifact ("Why does scope creep always start small?"), ask a speculative yes/no curiosity question with a soft payoff ("Can Claude Code query your database itself?"), or state the twist as a flat declarative fact up front ("Claude Code silently ignored your AGENTS.md file"), leaving nothing concrete left to resolve. In short, strong covers hook with a specific, personal technical pain point and an implied concrete answer, while weak covers are more abstract, speculative, or give the punchline away in the title itself.
