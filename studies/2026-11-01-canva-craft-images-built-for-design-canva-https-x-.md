## Pacing
Cut rate is not uniform (overall cuts_per_10s=6.8, beats_per_10s=5.1, moving_share=0.43 meaning nearly half the frames contain motion even in 'static' shots). It runs in three distinct speeds: (1) very fast, 0-3.3s, a cut roughly every 0.3-1.3s during the silent image montage; (2) slow, 4.4-14.7s, cuts every 1.7-4.5s while text cards and the brand name are read; (3) fast again, 20.1-31.6s, cuts roughly every 1-1.5s during the ranking-chart/demo-results section. This fast-slow-fast shape gives the eye a sprint, a breather to read/absorb the claim and name, then a sprint again through the proof. Loudness stays low (-19.8 to -16.3dBFS) for the first ~1.5s then jumps and holds steady around -6 to -9dBFS from ~2s to the end - a continuous music bed rather than voice driving the energy (speech only exists as one short line at 13.26s).

## Why it works
- Opens with zero text/voice and pure, fast (every 0.3-1.3s) scene variety across 7 unrelated beautiful shots - a pattern-interrupt that stops the scroll on visual novelty alone before any claim is made.
- Uses constantly-moving footage (cinemagraph-style: drifting mist, rolling dunes, shifting light, gradient shimmer - moving_share 0.43) even during 'static' text cards, so there are no dead/frozen frames to lose attention on.
- Deliberately slows the cut rate during the brand-name reveal (4.4-14.7s, cuts every ~2-4.5s) right after a frantic open, giving the eye a rest exactly when it needs to read and absorb the claim and name - classic hook/breathe/payoff rhythm.
- Places concrete, numbered social proof (a ranked leaderboard of competing AI labs) almost exactly at the midpoint (14.7s of 35.2s), converting attention earned by the visuals into credibility.
- Speeds back up for the demo-results section (13 cuts in ~17s) to end on a flashy, almost playful payoff (the dog-on-sofa generated image) rather than fading out quietly.
- A steady, unbroken music bed (-6 to -9dBFS from ~2s onward) smooths over every cut and keeps emotional energy constant through both slow and fast sections.

## What we could test
- **A text-free, voice-free rapid pre-hook montage (multiple different visuals in well under 1s each) before any hook text/claim appears, to earn attention through variety alone.**: Open a reel with 4-5 lightning-fast flashes (0.2-0.4s each) of different code/terminal/diff/screenshot snippets from the topic before the hook text card appears, instead of starting flat on the hook card. Test: Run this fast pre-roll flash-open in half of new reels and keep the current straight-to-hook-card open in the other half; compare % of viewers retained at the 3s mark and overall skip rate against the 76.9% median baseline.
- **Two-stage text reveal that withholds the specific name/subject: a vague setup line first, then the concrete claim/name a beat later, rather than showing the full hook sentence all at once.**: Split the hook card into two beats within the same 4-5s window: e.g. 1.5s of a vague curiosity line, then cut to the specific tool/trick name for the remaining time, instead of one static full-sentence card. Test: A/B half the reels with a single static hook card vs. a two-beat hook reveal; measure hook retention (viewers remaining at the end of the hook window, ~4-5s mark).
- **A simple ranked/numbered list or bar-chart graphic used as a mid-video credibility beat.**: On comparison-style topics, replace one of the three point-slides with a plain ranked list or bar chart (e.g. speed, cost, GitHub stars, pricing tiers) instead of only code/terminal screenshots. Test: For comparison-topic reels, use a chart/ranked-list slide in half and a code/terminal slide for the equivalent point in the other half; compare completion rate and watch time against the 3.9s median baseline.
- **A visible burst of rapid, varied output shots right after the main demo, to show volume/breadth of results rather than a single example.**: After the main terminal/diff demo slide, add a 1.5-2s flash montage of 3-4 different output screenshots (different results from the same tool/command) before moving to the question slide. Test: Add this post-demo flash montage in half of reels and omit it in the other half; compare retention through the final question slide and overall completion rate.
- **Uneven, three-phase pacing (fast open, slower explanatory middle, fast close) rather than a flat, uniform cut rate throughout.**: Deliberately vary cut density per segment: faster cuts on the hook and payoff/demo sections, slightly slower/held cuts on the explanation slides where the audio is doing more work. Test: Produce half the reels with the current near-uniform cut pace and half with this fast-slow-fast structure; compare median watch time against the current 3.9s baseline.
