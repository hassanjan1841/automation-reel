## Why it works
- An explicit numeric promise at second 0 ("under 60 seconds") plus 17 words crammed into the first 3 seconds front-loads information and sets a countdown the viewer can track, reducing early drop-off.
- The structure escalates specificity and stakes in order: third-party credibility screenshot → plain-language explanation → live demo with a real progress bar and real numbers → head-to-head comparison stat → bigger/flashier demo → CTA, so each beat answers "ok but does it actually work" a little more concretely.
- A real, measurable pause (loudness drops ~30dB from roughly -13dBFS to -42/-44dBFS at 25.1-26.8s, the video's longest_pause) lets the viewer watch an on-screen progress bar finish before the voiceover delivers the payoff number, turning a mundane loading bar into a mini cliffhanger.
- Text on screen during the proof section is mostly the real artifact being filmed (tweet text, lesson headers, product UI, result numbers) rather than added graphic overlays, so what the viewer reads doubles as evidence the claim is real.
- The ending text card ("JEV") echoes the opening card's cyan-box style, bookending the piece and reinforcing the one word/name the viewer is asked to act on (comment it) right at the CTA moment.

Test: On half of upcoming reels, raise voiceover pace/word-density by ~20-30% in just the hook card (still 2.6 wps elsewhere) and compare average watch time against reels with today's pacing.
- **A deliberate near-silent beat right before a payoff number, timed to a visual (progress bar / counter) finishing.**: On the terminal/code or diff slide that shows a result (e.g. a build finishing, a metric printing), drop the voiceover and most SFX for ~1s while the on-screen number/animation completes, then resume VO with the punch line stat. Test: On half the reels, insert a ~1s near-silent pause before the key metric reveal on slide 2 or 3; compare completion rate / watch time to reels with continuous VO through the reveal.
- **Escalating proof structure across the body: claim → explanation → live demo with numbers → comparison → bigger demo → CTA, rather than three same-weight points.**: Reorder the three point-slides so slide 1 states the capability, slide 2 shows a live terminal/code result with a real metric, and slide 3 is a head-to-head comparison or the most impressive example, before the closing question. Test: Run half the reels with today's flat three-point structure and half with an escalating order (feature → live result → comparison/biggest example); compare retention curve at each slide boundary.
- **Close on a text card that echoes the opening hook card's style/wording, bookending the video right before the CTA.**: Add a closing text card using the same color/typography as the opening hook card, restating the key term or promise just delivered, placed right before the closing question. - **Let text-heavy screenshots (code, terminal, docs) carry their own on-screen text rather than stacking extra graphic highlight boxes over already-legible content.**: On slides where the code/terminal screenshot is readable at speed, skip the extra highlight/callout box and let burnt-in captions alone carry narration, letting the artifact itself be the visual text.
