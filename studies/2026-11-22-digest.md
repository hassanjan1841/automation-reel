# What keeps working for others (9 recent studies)

Across the 9 studies, high-performing videos vary their cut rate (holding shots during explanation, saving fast punches for one climax), use deliberate near-silent beats for contrast, and close on a distinct text-only card/line rather than ending on bare spoken audio — none of which our reels currently do, since we punch every sentence uniformly and cut off right after the spoken question. They also open stronger (staged hook reveals, confident numeric claims, live/moving visuals instead of static cards) and add a quick varied-output montage before wrapping up, whereas our reels open on a single static hook card and go straight from point 3 to the question with no extra proof beat.

- **Uneven cut rate: hold shots steady during explanation and save fast punch-cuts for one concentrated climax, instead of punching on every single sentence.** (in 5 of 9 studies): Within each of the 3 point-slides, cut once at the slide change and hold through the explanation sentence(s); stack the punch-cuts (2-3 quick hits) only on point 3's payoff visual and/or the beat right before the closing question, instead of punching on every sentence across all three slides. Test: For half of new reels, remove mid-slide punch-cuts on point-slides 1 and 2 (cut only at slide boundaries) and concentrate extra punches on point 3; keep the current punch-per-sentence pacing on the other half. Compare median watch time and skip rate.
- **A dedicated text-only closing beat (echoed hook line, wordmark, or verbatim repeat of the last line) anchoring the ending, rather than letting the video finish on a bare spoken question alone.** (in 5 of 9 studies): After the closing question, hold a 1s text-only card — either the final question repeated on-screen in bold, or a one-line echo of the opening hook text — as a visual full stop before the video ends, instead of cutting out right as the question's audio finishes. Test: Add this 1s closing text card to half of new reels; let the other half end exactly as now, cutting off right after the spoken question. Compare drop-off in the last 2 seconds and overall skip rate.
- **A deliberate near-silent / quiet beat used as contrast, placed either right before a payoff or right after a loud opening.** (in 3 of 9 studies): Drop the voiceover and SFX for about 1-1.5s right before the point-3 visual lands (diff resolves, terminal finishes, output appears), letting the visual sit in near-silence before the punchline hits. Test: Add a 1-1.5s silent beat before the point-3 payoff in half of the reels; keep continuous VO/SFX through that moment in the other half. Compare watch time around the point-3 slide and overall completion.
- **Two-stage hook reveal: a vague/partial fragment appears first, then the full specific hook line settles a beat later, instead of popping the complete hook sentence instantly.** (in 2 of 9 studies): On the opening hook card, show a short partial phrase (e.g. the first few words) for a fraction of a second before the rest of the line fills in, rather than having the full hook sentence appear in one pop. Test: Build the hook card with this partial-then-full reveal on half of upcoming reels; keep the current instant full-text pop on the other half. Compare 3s watch-through rate (since current median watch time is only 3.9s).
- **Escalating three-point structure where each slide changes one variable in a clear direction (bigger proof, smaller cost, etc.) instead of three independent, equal-weight points.** (in 2 of 9 studies): Script the three point-slides as a visible progression on one artifact or metric (e.g. point 1: raw problem, point 2: partial fix, point 3: full working result/number), rather than three unrelated tips. Test: Write half of the next batch of scripts with this one-variable escalating structure across the 3 slides; keep the other half as independent equal-weight points as usual. Compare average watch time across the 3-slide section.
- **Cold open or mid-video reveal on a live, moving artifact (code typing, terminal running) instead of a static screenshot/text card.** (in 2 of 9 studies): Replace at least one static code/terminal screenshot per reel with a short live-typing animation of that same code/output, and consider pairing the hook text itself with an already-animating terminal instead of a plain static hook card. Test: In half of reels, swap the point-slide visuals (or the hook visual) from static screenshots to live-typing/animated versions; keep static screenshots in the other half. Compare skip rate in the first 5 seconds.
- **A confident, absolute or numeric claim in the hook (explicit promise/time-save/result) rather than a soft or question-style opener.** (in 2 of 9 studies): Write hook lines as a specific declarative claim with a number where possible (e.g. a time saved, a line-count reduced, a specific result), instead of a general teaser phrase. Test: Write half of upcoming hooks as explicit number/claim statements and half as the current teaser-style hooks; compare median watch time, since skip rate is currently very high (76.6%) and hook strength is the likely lever.
- **A brief wordless burst of multiple varied output shots placed right after the main demo/proof, to show breadth of results before wrapping up.** (in 2 of 9 studies): Right after point 3's main proof visual and before the closing question, insert a quick (under 2-3s) silent montage of 3-4 different output shots (terminal result, diff, rendered UI, chat reply) to signal breadth rather than a single example. Test: Add this short wordless output-montage before the closing question in half of new reels; go straight from point 3 to the question in the other half. Compare completion rate near the end of the reel.
