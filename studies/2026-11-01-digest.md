# What keeps working for others (7 recent studies)

The studied videos break uniformity in ways our reels don't: they vary cut pace and loudness instead of a flat per-sentence punch rhythm, withhold/stage the hook instead of revealing it instantly, turn the "three points" into one escalating or continuous artifact instead of three equal slides, and add a distinct closing beat (card or output montage) before the final CTA. Our reels currently show the full hook at once, punch on every sentence at a constant rate, treat the three point-slides as separate equal-weight items on static screenshots, and cut straight from slide 3 to the question — none of which match these recurring techniques.

- **Near-silent/loudness-drop beat used at a high-leverage moment (right before a payoff reveal, or as a contrast beat after a loud/fast opening)** (in 3 of 7 studies): Right before the number/result lands on slide 3 (e.g. a diff count, speed-up %, terminal result), cut the SFX and let the AI voiceover drop to a brief pause (~0.5-1s) while the visual (counter, progress bar, diff) finishes animating, then hit the payoff with the next punch and SFX back on. Test: In half of the next batch of reels, insert a ~0.5-1s silent beat (no SFX, voiceover pause) immediately before the slide-3 payoff; keep constant SFX/pacing in the other half. Compare watch time curve around that timestamp and overall completion rate.
- **A distinct closing card/beat (visual full-stop) inserted right before the CTA/question, rather than cutting straight from the last point-slide to it** (in 3 of 7 studies): Add a ~0.5-1s clean closing card — either a plain logo/wordmark card or a one-line text card echoing the opening hook's wording/style — between the last point-slide and the closing question, instead of jumping directly from slide 3 to the question card. Test: Add this closing card before the question in half of the next reels; leave the other half with the current direct cut from slide 3 to the question. Compare drop-off in the final 2-3s and overall completion rate.
- **Uneven cut pace matched to content density (hold during explanation, extra punches only on fast lists/climax) instead of a flat one-punch-per-sentence rate** (in 3 of 7 studies): Within the point-slides, hold the visual steady for the sentence that explains an idea (no punch), then add 2-3 quick extra punches only when the voiceover rattles off a fast list or comparison (e.g. multiple tool names or before/after numbers), making the cut rate uneven rather than constant. Test: Apply this uneven pacing (hold-then-burst) to half of the next reels; keep the current one-punch-per-sentence flat pacing on the other half. Compare watch time and skip rate between the two groups.
- **Two-stage hook that withholds the specific subject (vague/partial line first, concrete claim or name a beat later) instead of showing the full hook sentence instantly** (in 2 of 7 studies): Within the existing 4-5s hook card window, show a vague setup line first (e.g. 'Most devs miss this one setting') for ~1.5s, then have the card update/reveal the specific tool, number, or claim for the remainder, instead of putting the whole hook sentence up in one shot. Test: Run half the next reels with the current single-shot full hook card and half with the two-stage reveal (same total 4-5s duration). Compare skip rate in the first 3s between the two groups.
- **Escalating/continuous arc replacing three same-weight, unrelated point-slides** (in 2 of 7 studies): Make the three point-slides track one evolving artifact (same file/terminal/diff shown broken → mid-fix → fixed, or claim → explanation → bigger demo) with rising stakes, so slide 3 pays off what slides 1-2 set up, instead of three standalone tips. Test: For half of the next reels, rewrite the body as one continuous before/during/after sequence on a single artifact; keep the other half as three separate standalone points. Compare median watch time and skip rate between the two groups.
- **Live/moving artifact (typing cursor, animating terminal) in place of a static screenshot** (in 2 of 7 studies): On at least one point-slide, replace the static code/terminal/diff screenshot with a live-typing cursor or line-by-line terminal output animation synced to the voiceover, so the code/result appears to happen in real time rather than being shown as a frozen image. Test: In half the next reels, animate the slide-2 code/terminal visual as live-typing; in the other half keep the current static screenshot. Compare watch time retention at that slide's timestamp.
- **A short wordless montage of multiple varied real outputs as a proof beat, placed near the end before the closing question** (in 2 of 7 studies): Before the closing question, add a 1-2s wordless montage of 3-4 quick, different real outputs from the same workflow (e.g. different terminal results, different UI states, different generated files) instead of ending on a single static final point-slide. Test: Add this proof montage before the question in half of the next reels; keep the current single final-slide-to-question transition in the other half. Compare skip rate in the final 3-5s and completion rate.
