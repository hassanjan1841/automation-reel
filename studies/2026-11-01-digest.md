# What keeps working for others (5 recent studies)

Across these studies, strong-performing videos stage their hook text in pieces instead of popping it fully at once, drop to near-silence for a beat right before a payoff/reveal, add one extra transitional beat between the last point and the CTA rather than cutting straight to it, and break up uniform per-sentence cutting (holding one idea longer, or escalating stakes instead of three flat equal points). Our reels do none of this: the hook card shows the full sentence instantly, every sentence gets an equal camera punch, three same-weight points cut straight into the closing question with continuous audio, and 77.3% of viewers skip at a median of 3.9s — right in the hook/first-point window these patterns are built to hold attention through.

- **A brief (0.5-1s) near-silent beat (no VO, no SFX) placed immediately before a payoff number/result/reveal, rather than talking straight through it.** (in 3 of 5 studies): Right before the result/output on whichever point lands the key proof (e.g. the final terminal output, a before/after diff resolving, or a stat in point 3), cut VO and SFX for ~0.5-1s so only the visual sits there a beat before the payoff text/visual appears. Test: In half the next batch of reels, insert this 0.5-1s silent beat right before the payoff moment; in the other half keep continuous audio through it as now. Compare watch-through rate past that point and completion rate.
- **Insert one extra short beat (tease, echo of the hook, or brief closing card) between the end of the last point and the closing question, instead of cutting straight from point 3 into the CTA.** (in 3 of 5 studies): After slide 3, add a ~1s transitional card before the question — e.g. a one-line forward tease ("next: how to automate X") or a short line that echoes the opening hook's wording — rather than jumping directly from the last point slide to the question. Test: In half the reels, add this extra pre-question beat; in the other half cut directly from point 3 to the question as currently done. Compare watch time in the last 3-4s and reply/comment rate on the question.
- **Reveal the hook text in two stages (a partial fragment synced to the first clause of VO, then the full line completing a moment later) instead of popping the complete hook sentence in one go.** (in 2 of 5 studies): On the opening hook card, show only the first clause/fragment of the hook as the VO starts, then let the rest of the sentence land ~0.5-1s later (still inside the existing 4-5s hook window), instead of displaying the whole hook text instantly. Test: For half the reels, split the hook card into a 2-stage reveal (partial then full); for the other half keep the current single full-pop hook card. Compare the 0-5s skip rate between groups.
- **Hold a single visual through one explained idea without cutting (reserving per-sentence/per-example cutting for list-style or payoff moments) instead of punching the camera on every sentence uniformly.** (in 2 of 5 studies): Pick whichever of the three points is a single continuous idea (e.g. one piece of code or one terminal flow being built/fixed) and keep one visual on screen through that point's full VO without a punch-cut, saving fast per-sentence cuts for a point that lists multiple short examples or for the final payoff slide. Test: In half the reels, remove the per-sentence punch-cut on the designated 'single idea' point (hold the visual through it); in the other half keep the current camera-punch-per-sentence on all three points. Compare the drop-off curve during that segment.
- **Break the flat 'three equal-weight points then end' shape by escalating the final point's stakes/result size or inserting a brief false-ending beat followed by one more point before the real close.** (in 2 of 5 studies): Make point 3 visibly bigger or higher-stakes than points 1 and 2 (e.g. small fix -> bigger fix -> full before/after comparison), or add a quick 'that's it' style pause after point 2 immediately followed by one more short bonus point before the question. Test: In half the reels, restructure so point 3 is a clear escalation (or add the false-ending-plus-bonus-point beat); in the other half keep three equal-weight points as structured now. Compare full-video completion rate.
