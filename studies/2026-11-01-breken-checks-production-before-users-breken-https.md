## Why it works
- Cold black open plus an 'unfolding UI message' at 0.5-1s (rather than an instant full headline) creates a 1-second curiosity gap before the viewer even knows what kind of video this is.
- Restyling the exact same short phrase three times in under 3 seconds (strike-through, particle-built, glitch) keeps the screen visually new every ~1s even though almost no new information is being delivered yet — this likely protects retention during the window before the payoff arrives.
- Mixing stock cinematic inserts (eye close-up at 5.3s, crowd/portrait montage at 15.3s) between text cards breaks the rhythm of 'just words on black' and raises production value, which plausibly reads as higher-stakes/more credible than a plain text-card video.
- The three-step escalating claim ('could calculate' → 'could communicate' → 'could think') builds an obvious mini-arc with only one or two words changing per beat, letting the viewer predict the pattern and feel rewarded when 'we are teaching them' lands as the turn.
- The loudness curve does real narrative work: it swells to its loudest point around the 17-20s cut cluster, then decays all the way to near-silence (-45.4 dBFS) right before the 'Thanks for watching' line — the silence itself signals 'something is about to happen,' which likely keeps people watching through what looks like a premature ending.
- The fake 'Thanks for watching' at 25.5s (roughly the midpoint, not the end) is a retention trick: viewers who assume the video is over and are about to swipe get surprised that it continues, which can re-earn attention right as natural drop-off would occur.
- Because the real content runs to 52.76s with a second, true 'Thanks for watching' at the very end, the video uses a false summit/second climb structure instead of one straight build, which is unusual for short-form and likely contributes to outsized watch time relative to a single straight-through hook-to-payoff edit.
