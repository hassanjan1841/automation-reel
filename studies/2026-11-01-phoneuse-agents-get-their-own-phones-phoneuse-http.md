## Pacing
Mixed-speed cutting, not uniformly fast: cuts_per_10s is a moderate 4.3 overall, but beats_per_10s (6.3) and moving_share (0.71) show that even within held shots there's near-constant internal motion (hand gestures, glowing/floating icons, camera push on the rooftop shots) — so it reads as 'always alive' without needing a hard cut every second. The video deliberately holds its first shot a full 2.9s (longer than the ~1-2s a typical fast ad would hold) to let the hook land, goes almost cut-free and voice-silent for the 2.9-9.0s product-demo insert (the single longest pause in the track, 6.2s), then compresses into a genuinely fast montage (6 cuts in ~4.5s) only for the capability list around 26.6-31.1s, before relaxing again toward the CTA. So pacing is shaped around the message (slow for the claim, silent for the demo, fast for the proof-list, calm for the close), not a flat rhythm.

## Why it works
- The opening shot pairs a spoken claim with literal visual 'evidence' (phones + app icons) already on screen, so viewers don't have to wait for a reveal to understand the premise — claim and proof are simultaneous.
- Captions build in clause-sized chunks synced to speech rather than dumping the full sentence at once, which forces the eye to keep tracking the screen for ~2.4s instead of reading everything in a glance and looking away.
- A problem/contrast line ('AI can already use computers, but the world still wants phones') is inserted before the brand name, creating a small information gap that the 'introducing PhoneUse' reveal then closes — classic tension-then-release structure.
- The 6.2s voice-silent demo insert (2.9-9.0s) is a deliberate pattern break: after a loud, fast hook, dropping to near-silence with just a UI mockup is itself attention-grabbing because it's different from what just happened, and it still communicates product behavior visually.
- The rapid six-cuts-in-4.5s montage lands exactly when the VO is listing multiple concrete, named use-cases (Uber, TikTok, insurance app, 2FA) — cut rate matches information rate, so the viewer is never asked to sit on one static image while new claims pile up.
- A sub-second cultural reference (archival keynote flash) is cheap to process and rewards recognition instantly, adding a beat of novelty without needing new explanation.
- The CTA repeats the exact spoken domain as on-screen text against a plain background, removing any ambiguity about what to do next right before the video ends.

## What we could test
- **Build the hook caption in synced clause-sized chunks (word-group by word-group, timed to the voiceover) instead of showing the full hook sentence in one pop.**: On the 4-5s hook card, split the hook line into 2-3 phrases that append on screen as the AI voiceover says each phrase, instead of the full sentence appearing instantly. Test: Run progressive clause-reveal hook captions on half of new reels, full-sentence-at-once hook captions on the other half; compare median watch time and skip rate in the first 4s.
- **Open with a problem/contrast statement ('X already exists, but Y gap remains') before naming the tool or technique, instead of leading straight with the feature.**: Write the hook card as a tension line (what's commonly believed vs. what's actually missing) rather than a direct 'Here's how to do X' statement, with the reveal slide resolving it. Test: A/B hook phrasing: contrast-style hook vs. direct-claim hook, split across reels on the same topic, compare 3s retention.
- **Match cut rate to information density: hold one visual while a single idea is explained, but cut once per named example when the voiceover rattles off a fast list.**: In the three point-slides, when the AI voiceover lists several quick examples (e.g., three commands or three outputs), cut the visual once per named item instead of holding one static screenshot for the whole slide. Test: On half the reels, add one extra cut per named example inside a list-heavy slide; keep the other half at one visual per slide. Compare watch-time drop-off at that slide's timestamp.
- **Insert a brief (1-2s) voiceover-silent beat where only the on-screen visual and a sound effect carry the message, as a pattern break after a fast/loud opening.**: After the hook card, let the first point-slide's visual (terminal/diff) sit for 1-2s with only an SFX (keystroke/whoosh) and no narration before the voiceover resumes explaining it. Test: Add a silent SFX-only beat right after the hook on half of reels; keep continuous voiceover on the other half; compare retention curve at that exact second.
- **Animate a live-typing cursor into a code/terminal/command visual instead of showing a static screenshot, to simulate the action happening in real time.**: For the code or terminal point-slide, animate the command or prompt being typed character by character (synced to a keystroke SFX) instead of cutting to a pre-filled static screenshot. Test: Use live-typing animation on the code slide for half the reels vs. static code screenshot for the other half; compare completion rate past that slide.

## Not for us
- Talking-head presenter on screen for the entire video, including close-up reaction shots and hand gestures used as the primary visual. (No faces or people on screen.)
- Archival footage of a real person (Steve Jobs keynote clip) spliced in as a reference/joke. (No people on screen, and reusing someone else's filmed footage/likeness counts as copying rather than an original pattern.)
- Continuous ambient/music-like score underlying the voiceover across the whole runtime (loudness track shows a steady musical bed rather than voice-only levels during pauses). (No music — this is a guess based on the loudness curve staying elevated even during the silent 2.9-9.0s voiceover gap, which is more consistent with a music bed than with sound effects alone.)
- Making concrete, specific product-capability claims (booking Uber, completing 2FA, navigating a named insurance app) shown via staged UI mockups presented as real agent behavior. (No invented/fake results — if we ever showed a capability demo, it would need to be a real output from our own tools, not a staged mockup presented as fact.)
