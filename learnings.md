# What holds our viewers (updated by learn.py)

- Open on the proof: the code or output that shows the point is on screen from frame 0, never a text card alone. (proven)
- When the result appears, hold a short silent beat so it lands before the voice names it. (proven)
- End on a direct question to the viewer, never a comment-for-a-guide freebie offer. (proven)
- Voiceover line 1 is dense: 12 to 14 spoken words, the whole promise said fast (it is played a little faster too); the alternatives' spoken lines are 12 to 14 words as well. (proven)
- The ending asks the viewer to send the reel to one developer who needs it: voiceover line 5 says so naturally ("Send this to the friend who still trusts that header."), and the cta is a short question that points at that person ("Know a dev who still *believes* this?"). (proven)
- Before/after hook_type reels average skip_vs_usual -3.2 versus +3.1 for untagged hooks (9 vs 14 reels); favor the before/after hook structure when the topic fits.
- Ai-pillar reels average skip_vs_usual -0.8 (5 reels) and timely-pillar +0.9 (9 reels); freelance-pillar reels average +7.7 (8 reels); favor ai or timely topics.
- Myth vs fact reels average skip_vs_usual -12.4 and watched_vs_usual +0.081 (6 reels), versus Freelance playbook's +8.0 and -0.037 (6 reels); favor myth-busting topics over freelance-advice topics.
- Mistake-type hooks cut skip rate far more than untagged hooks: skip_vs_usual -12.0 vs +5.2, watched_vs_usual +0.114 vs +0.006 (6 vs 14 reels).
- Diff visuals beat plain code visuals: skip_vs_usual -7.7 vs -4.0, watched_vs_usual +0.051 vs +0.032 (10 vs 40 reels).
