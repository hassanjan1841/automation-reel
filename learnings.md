# What holds our viewers (updated by learn.py)

- Skip rate is the biggest problem right now: 82-84% of viewers leave in the first 3 seconds on all 3 reels. Before anything else, fix the opening hook/first frame — topic and pillar choices matter less than this.
- Timely/news reels (2 reels, early sign) had a slightly lower skip rate (82.4% vs 83.8%) and longer watch time (3923ms vs 2973ms) than the single concept reel. Worth trying a few more news-style reels, but 2 reels isn't enough to call it a trend yet.
- All 3 reels used text-only visuals, so there's no comparison data on other formats. Test reels with a screen recording, code or a 3D diagram next to see if they hold attention better than text cards.
- Saves and shares are almost non-existent (1 save, 0 shares across all 3 reels). None of the current hooks are giving people a reason to save/share — add a clear 'save this' or 'send to a friend' prompt at the end.
- Average watch time is under 4 seconds on every reel, even the one with the most views. The opening needs a stronger pull (a question, a visual surprise, or the payoff teased immediately) before any explanation starts.
- Sample size is tiny (3 reels, all posted the same day). Don't commit to one pillar or series yet — keep posting a mix of concept and timely/news reels for another week or two before picking a lane.
- The reel with the most reach (id 29, 138 views, 'Next.js RCE fix') also had one of the lower skip rates — an early sign that urgent/breaking-news phrasing may work, but confirm with more news reels before relying on it.
- Zero comments across all reels. Try ending with a direct question in the caption or on-screen text to invite replies, since right now nothing is prompting people to engage beyond watching.
- Reels tagged hook_style: question averaged a worse skip rate than reels run with no test (skip_vs_usual +4.1 vs -0.7, 5 reels each).
- Favor freebie-style CTAs over question CTAs: freebie reels average skip_vs_usual +0.1 vs +3.0 for question reels (11 vs 7 reels).
